Most buyers I meet in Orange County are not short on effort. They are short on a straight answer about what they qualify for. They have a number sitting in savings, and they want to know whether that number is enough. The names arrive as an alphabet instead: CalHFA, FHA, VA, HomeReady, Home Possible, and whatever their city is running this year. Each one has its own income limit, its own paperwork, and its own catch.

Here is the part that gets buried. Down payment assistance is usually a loan in second position behind your mortgage. It helps you buy. It doesn't go away when you do. That one fact changes how you should plan, and it is what I explain most often before we look at a single listing.

## What first-time buyer programs are available in Orange County?

In Orange County, help comes from four places: CalHFA state programs, including the MyHome junior loan, FHA loans at 3.5% down, VA loans at zero down for eligible veterans, and conventional programs like HomeReady and Home Possible at 3% down. Cities and counties add their own programs, and those open and close with funding.

That's the short answer. The rest of this page is about the catches, because the catches decide whether you can actually buy.

## The four buckets of help

Almost everything you hear about falls into four groups. They're not interchangeable, and some stack.

| Program type | Typical down payment | Who it fits | Main catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| State or local down payment assistance, such as CalHFA MyHome or a city program | Can cover part or all of your down payment when paired with a first mortgage | Buyers under the program income limit with steady income and thin savings | It's a lien with repayment terms, and it doesn't fix debt-to-income |
| FHA-insured first mortgage | 3.5% with a 580 or higher credit score in HUD's handbook | Buyers with modest savings or imperfect credit | Mortgage insurance premiums, and lenders may add stricter overlays |
| VA-backed loan | Zero down for eligible borrowers | Veterans, active duty, and some surviving spouses | A funding fee typically applies, and the property must meet VA standards |
| Conventional low-down programs, such as HomeReady or Home Possible | 3% for eligible buyers | Buyers inside the program income limits with reasonable credit | Income limits, mortgage insurance, and tighter condo rules |

The first row isn't a loan type. It's a layer that sits on top of one of the other three. That's where most of the confusion starts.

One warning about searching. Plenty of results for Orange County down payment assistance describe Orange County, Florida. Check the address on the program page before the income limit.

The rules come from the agencies, not from a listing agent. The [VA publishes its home loan requirements](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/), HUD states the [FHA down payment minimum](https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/loans) and publishes the full policy in [Handbook 4000.1](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/housing/sfh/handbook_4000-1), and [Fannie Mae](https://www.fanniemae.com/singlefamily/homeready) and Freddie Mac publish the rules for HomeReady and Home Possible.

Two details worth knowing if your savings are thin. Fannie Mae lists HomeReady at down payments as low as 3% with no minimum personal contribution, which means gift funds or assistance can cover the whole 3%. And the VA's own figures show that nearly 90% of VA-backed loans are made with no down payment at all, so the obstacle for most veterans is not the down payment. It is the closing costs and the reserves behind them.

## How CalHFA MyHome actually works

MyHome is the program buyers ask about most. The mechanics matter more than the marketing page.

- It is a deferred-payment junior loan. With a CalHFA government first mortgage, the cap is the lesser of 3.5% of the purchase price or the appraised value. With a CalHFA conventional first mortgage, the cap is the lesser of 3% of the purchase price or the appraised value.
- It can go toward the down payment, closing costs, or both. CalHFA's wording is "and/or," so the cap does not grow when you use it for both.
- Payments are deferred. You generally make no monthly payment on the second loan. Interest may still accrue while nothing is due, so read the note rather than the brochure.
- It comes due at sale, refinance, or payoff, and the home has to stay your primary residence while the loan is open.
- You have to complete homebuyer education. Only one occupying first-time borrower on the loan needs to complete it, so a couple doesn't both have to enroll.
- CalHFA accepts exactly one online course: eHome's eight-hour class, listed at $100, which includes a one-on-one counseling session. CalHFA says other online courses, including Frameworks and HomeView, are rejected because they skip that session. A live class through a HUD-approved agency also works, at whatever that agency charges.
- Non-occupant co-borrowers are not allowed.
- The property has to be a single-family, one-unit residence. Condos and planned unit developments are allowed when they meet the first mortgage's project guidelines. Double-wide manufactured homes are permitted. Single-wide are not.

Two things CalHFA does not do. It doesn't lend to you directly, so you apply through an approved lender. And it doesn't publish one income limit for everyone. Limits vary by county and by program, so check the current [income limits page](https://www.calhfa.ca.gov/homeownership/limits/index.htm) for the program you are using instead of trusting a number from a forum.

One more caution. Program terms, rates, and funding change. The [MyHome program page](https://www.calhfa.ca.gov/homebuyer/programs/myhome.htm) is the source to check in the same week you apply, not a month before.

## What Orange County buyers get wrong about assistance

### Mistake one: treating it as free money

It's a loan. It's recorded against the property. Interest may accrue. When you sell or refinance, it gets paid, usually before you see the proceeds. Some assistance is written as a grant, but most of what you will be offered is a loan. If someone describes assistance as free, ask for the note.

### Mistake two: forgetting the second lien

Two things happen when you add a second loan. Your lender may count the obligation in your debt-to-income ratio. And your refinance later has to clear it. Buyers plan the purchase and forget the exit. Ask what the payoff looks like in year five, not just what you pay at closing.

### Mistake three: shopping before pre-approval

You can't know which programs you fit until a lender reviews your file. Pre-qualification is a conversation. Pre-approval is a reviewed file. In a market where well-priced homes draw multiple offers, the second one is what gets your offer read. Start there, then tour. The [step-by-step buyer's guide](/buyers/) walks through the rest in order.

### Mistake four: assuming it covers the gap

Assistance is capped as a percentage of the price. It isn't a bucket that fills whatever hole is left between your savings and your total cash to close. Closing costs, prepaid taxes and insurance, reserves, and moving costs are usually still yours.

## What you actually need in cash

Four numbers, and they're not the same number.

**Down payment.** A percentage of the price, set by the loan program. 3.5% for FHA, 3% for eligible conventional programs, zero for eligible VA borrowers. Assistance can supply part of it.

**Closing costs.** Lender fees, escrow, title, recording, and prepaid items. A few percent of the price is the usual range in my experience. Your Loan Estimate, which the lender must send within three business days, itemizes them.

**Reserves.** Money left after closing. Some programs and lenders require a set number of months of payments in reserve. Even when it is not required, arriving at closing with nothing left is how the first year of ownership turns into a credit card balance.

**Insurance.** Homeowners insurance has to be bound before closing, with the lender named as mortgagee, and escrow collects the first year as a prepaid item. On an older home, or anything in a higher fire or flood risk area, that quote can move late in the process. Start it early rather than in the final week.

Here is the arithmetic on a price I picked for this example. It is not a market figure, not a quote, and not a prediction about any specific home in Orange County. Assume a $700,000 price and an FHA loan at 3.5% down:

- Down payment: $24,500
- Closing costs at 2% to 3%, as an assumption: $14,000 to $21,000
- Cash to close before reserves: about $38,500 to $45,500

Now add MyHome at 3.5%, which is $24,500 on this price. It can cover the down payment. It cannot cover the down payment and the closing costs both, because the cap does not move when you spread it across two uses. Your own cash still has to handle the $14,000 to $21,000, plus reserves and insurance.

That's the conversation I have most often. The program closes part of the gap. It doesn't close all of it. If the remaining number is still out of reach, the answer is usually a lower price, a different city, or more time. A [Garden Grove home](/garden-grove-ca-home-for-sale/) or a [Westminster home](/westminster-ca-home-for-sale/) can change the arithmetic without changing your loan. A bigger loan only changes the payment.

## A realistic sequence

1. Check your credit. Pull all three reports and dispute anything wrong. Errors take time to clear.
2. Get pre-approved with a lender who actually runs CalHFA and knows which local programs are open. Ask directly.
3. Take the homebuyer education course if the program requires it. CalHFA does, the eHome class is listed at $100, and the certificate has to be in the file before closing.
4. Set two ceilings before you tour anything: the monthly payment you can live with, and the cash you can lose without wrecking your savings.
5. Shop a price band, not a wish list. If condos are in play, confirm the project is approved before you write an offer.
6. Offer, escrow, appraisal, loan approval, final walkthrough, keys. Escrow commonly runs around 30 days, though the loan type and the contract decide.

## Where the programs stop helping

### High-price markets

Assistance scales with the price, so a higher-priced county can mean more dollars available. CalHFA dropped its sales price limits back in 2020, so the price of the home is not the gate. Income is. The first mortgage still has to be one you can carry, and a larger second loan does not make the monthly payment comfortable.

### Condo project approval

FHA and VA both care about the project, not just the unit. Owner-occupancy ratios, open litigation, budget reserves, and certification status can all stop a loan. CalHFA condos also have to meet the first mortgage's guidelines. I have watched buyers fall for a unit their financing could not touch. Check before you offer.

### Self-employed income

Qualifying income comes from your tax returns, not your deposits. Write-offs that lower your taxable income also lower the income the lender can use. A strong year can still fail to qualify if the return is thin. Expect more documentation and a longer timeline, and check the program income limit early.

### Non-occupant co-borrowers

CalHFA doesn't allow them. If a parent plans to go on the loan to help you qualify, that plan and CalHFA assistance may not fit together. A gift of funds is a different path with its own documentation rules. Ask before you build the budget around it.
