I work on Santa Ana Canyon Road. My office is at 5241 E. Santa Ana Canyon Road, Suite 100, so I am in Anaheim Hills most days, driving the streets between the 91 and the ridgelines.

People rarely ask me what the area is like in general. They ask whether one specific house on one specific street will work for their family. Those are different questions, and the second one is where the work is.

## Who Anaheim Hills fits, and who it does not

Fits: you want more house and more land than central Orange County tends to offer at your budget. You like living above the valley floor, with open space close by. You want a strong public school option. You are fine with two cars.

Does not fit: you want to walk to coffee. You want a flat lot, a level pool, and a lawn. You want a new build with a clubhouse. You want a payment you can forecast without reading a tax bill and an insurance quote first.

Neither list is a judgment. It is a filter. Most buyers relax when they see the streets, or realize that a flat lot in Orange suits them better.

## Where Anaheim Hills actually is

Anaheim Hills is the eastern, hilly part of the City of Anaheim. It is not a separate city, which surprises people. It borders Yorba Linda, the City of Orange and unincorporated county land, and those lines do not always follow a street.

Three roads carry you in. The 91 runs along the Santa Ana Canyon corridor on the southern edge. The 55 meets the 91 west of here. The 241 toll road comes up from the south and lands on the 91 near the canyon.

Price that toll before you fall for a house. The 241 is the direct line toward Irvine and the 5, and when traffic is bad it is the difference between a predictable trip and an unpredictable one. It is charged per trip, and a household driving it twice a day pays monthly. If your job is west, it does not help you.

Two ZIP codes cover most of it. 92807 runs through the western and central streets, 92808 through the eastern side toward the canyon. Do not read either one as a price tag or a school boundary. It is a first filter, not a rule.

## The neighborhoods, roughly

These names are informal. Anaheim Hills has no official neighborhood boundaries, and no two agents draw the map the same way.

**Lower canyon streets near the 91 and Santa Ana Canyon Road.** Older housing, some condos, the shortest run to shopping. Convenient, with traffic noise on some streets. Stand outside at 5pm before you call it quiet.

**The tracts on the western and central side.** Sidewalks, mature trees, similar plans up and down the street, smaller lots than the ridgeline. Where a lot of move-up families land.

**The ridgeline and upper streets.** Bigger lots, longer driveways, a mix of tract and custom homes, and views that depend on which way the house faces. Not every address sold as a view property has one.

**The eastern side toward the canyon.** Later phases mixed in with older streets, and a higher chance the house sits inside an association. Ask either way.

**The edges toward Yorba Linda and Orange.** Cross a street here and you can change city, school district, tax line, or all three.

Some listings marketed as Anaheim Hills sit just outside the city limits, in unincorporated Orange County. That changes who provides services and sometimes which district the address feeds. Ask for the APN and the jurisdiction in writing.

## Schools: what to check and how

Most Anaheim Hills addresses are served by Orange Unified School District. [OUSD's Anaheim Hills page](https://www.orangeusd.org/our-schools/anaheim-hills) lists seven schools for the area: Canyon High School, El Rancho Charter Middle School, Anaheim Hills, Imperial, Canyon Rim and Crescent elementary schools, and Running Springs Academy.

Canyon High is at 220 S. Imperial Highway in Anaheim. El Rancho Charter Middle is at 181 S. Del Giorgio Drive. Canyon Rim Elementary is at 1090 S. The Highlands Drive.

What matters more: assignment is set by the specific address, not the neighborhood name and not the ZIP code. A house described as being in Anaheim Hills can feed a different elementary school than the house behind it. Look the address up in the district's school locator and call OUSD to confirm, before you offer and again before contingencies come off.

I am not going to tell you a school is the reason to buy a house. Scores move and programs change. The assignment is checkable, so check it.

## The hillside premium and the hillside tax

You pay for elevation and view on the way in, then pay again in maintenance and insurance. Neither is a reason to avoid the area. Both are reasons to study the lot as closely as the kitchen.

**Slope and drainage.** Driveway grade tells you about winter. A steep drive sheds water fast and needs a drain at the bottom, or the water finds the garage. Walk the lot after rain, or look for evidence: staining at the base of stucco, fresh paint low on a wall, new sod in one patch, a French drain with no visible outlet. On a hillside, water usually goes to the neighbor below.

**Retaining walls.** Who built it, was it permitted and engineered, where is the property line, who maintains it. A wall that fails is one of the larger bills a hillside house can hand you, and a home warranty rarely covers it.

**Roof geometry.** Complex roofs with multiple valleys give water more places to get in, and some carriers ask about roof age before they quote.

**Insurance.** Get a quote on the specific address before you remove your inspection contingency. Ask whether the fire zone, roof age, brush clearance or the property's claim history affects the price.

**Wildfire.** The city maps three fire hazard severity zones, moderate, high and very high, and which one applies depends on where the parcel sits. The state map from CAL FIRE, the county map and an insurer's own model are three different things. Start with [OCFA's severity zone maps](https://ocfa.org/residents/fhsz/), then look at the vegetation and the access road, then talk to a broker.

## HOA and Mello-Roos

Both are parcel-specific, and both move your monthly number.

Some Anaheim Hills homes are in associations and many are not. If this one is, read the package, not the dues figure. HOA reserve study, budget, board minutes, pending litigation, special assessments, rental restrictions, and what the association actually maintains. On a hillside that last answer can include slopes and drainage structures. Your lender reads the same documents and may price the loan differently.

Mello-Roos is a Community Facilities District special tax, billed under special assessment charges. Some newer tracts here carry one. Older tracts often predate that financing, but I would not buy on the assumption. Pull the bill by APN, or start with [the county's parcel tax map](https://mello.ocgov.com/) and read the lines. It counts in your debt-to-income, so it changes what you can borrow. The [mortgage page](/mortgage/) has the calculators to run both numbers.

## Commute and daily life

I am not going to give you drive times. They depend on the hour, the direction and whether there is a crash in the canyon. A number on a page is worse than no number.

The shape of it: the 91 is the main east-west artery and its reputation is earned. Peak hours are slow in both directions, and the express lanes are a paid alternative, not a free fix. The 55 runs south toward Costa Mesa and the 405. The 241 runs south toward Irvine and the 5. Imperial Highway and Santa Ana Canyon Road carry the errands.

Daily life is car dependent. The centers along Santa Ana Canyon Road and Imperial Highway cover groceries and the usual needs. Oak Canyon Nature Center has trails in the canyon, run by the city. Anaheim Hills Golf Club is an 18-hole par-71 course with daily public tee times on Nohl Ranch Road. Check posted hours before you plan around either one.

## How Anaheim Hills compares to Yorba Linda, Orange and Villa Park

I get this comparison constantly. There is no winner, only a fit.

**Yorba Linda** shares the hills and in places shares a street. What changes is the city, often the school district, and the street pattern. Parts of it are flatter, with more horse property and larger flat lots. If you want a corral and a pad for a shop, that is a different search than a view lot on a slope.

**Orange** has older stock, more walkable pockets near the old town, and flatter lots. You trade hillside views for a sidewalk and a shorter walk to dinner, and take on the inspection list older construction brings. Boundaries there need the same address-level check. The [Orange page](/orange-ca-home-for-sale/) covers that side of the line.

**Villa Park** is small, flat, and built on larger lots, with very little turnover. When something comes up, it is a specific house, not a category.

The practical difference for move-up buyers is inventory. Anaheim Hills produces more houses to compare in a month, which means more data and more room to negotiate. The [Anaheim page](/anaheim-ca-home-for-sale/) covers the rest of the city.

## What to do before you make an offer here

Eight things.

1. **Widen the inspection scope.** General inspection, sewer scope, chimney, roof, deck, retaining walls, and a drainage opinion if you see movement or water. On a hillside the outside matters as much as the inside.
2. **Get an insurance quote on the address.** Before contingencies come off. Ask about fire zone, roof age and brush clearance.
3. **Pull the permit history.** Additions, walls, decks, pools, panel upgrades, anything structural. Unpermitted work becomes your problem after closing.
4. **Read the HOA package.** Not the dues summary. Reserves, minutes, litigation, special assessments, rental caps.
5. **Read the actual tax bill.** Both installments, all special assessments, against whatever the listing claimed.
6. **Confirm the school assignment with OUSD.** For that address, before you offer, and again before you close.
7. **Walk the drainage.** Base of walls, top and bottom of retaining walls, swales, the neighbor's runoff direction. Look in the rain if the timing allows.
8. **Check the fire exposure.** Zone map, vegetation, access, and what the seller has done for defensible space.
